# Audit — 2026-09-12

A second full audit, at `d0558b8`, two days after [the first](AUDIT-2026-09-10.md) and one
day after the columnar/row path was removed. Same six questions: code quality, organisation,
tests, benchmarks, dead code, developer experience.

**Method.** Five readers swept the repository in parallel — one per dimension, with dead
code folded into code quality — and every claim they returned was re-verified here before
being written down. Verification means: the probe was compiled and run, the grep was
re-executed, the benchmark arm was invoked, the document was opened. Where a finding is
recorded without that, it says so.

The developer-experience pass built a real consumer package against `.package(path:)` and
compiled twenty-odd deliberately wrong schemas rather than reasoning about what the compiler
would say.

**Verdict.** The first audit found the surface was weaker than the internals. That is no
longer the story. This one found something narrower and more uncomfortable: **the four
worst defects were all created in the previous 48 hours, by the same work that fixed other
things, and every one of them was invisible to the checks that exist.**

- The documented flagship example does not compile, and has not for as long as it has had a
  `Date` field in it.
- The Documentation CI job was red on `main`, broken by yesterday's removal.
- A benchmark gate printed `GATE FAILED` and exited 0.
- The performance journal quoted a number no arm printed any more — in the same file, four
  hundred lines below an entry explaining that exact failure mode.

None of that is a code-quality problem. The code is in good order. It is a **verification**
problem: the things that check this repository do not check the things a reader actually
touches.

## 1b. Two more, from the reader that came back late

A sixth reader returned after the rest, reporting three CRITICALs it had **not verified
itself** and flagging a calibration problem in its own source. All three were tested here
before anything was believed. That was the right instinct: one was real and severe, one was
real and overstated by two orders of magnitude, one did not reproduce at all.

**A 50-byte binary plist crashed the process — REAL, fixed.** `BinaryPlist.swift`'s UID arm
was `Int64(readBE(p + 1, width))` with `width` up to 8. A UID with the top bit set does not
fit `Int64` and the conversion traps. Reproduced with `87 FF FF FF FF FF FF FF FF`: `Fatal
error: Not enough bits to represent the passed value`, in **release as well as debug**.

The integer arm three cases above gets the identical situation right, and says why in a
comment — *"a number read as a different number is the one failure a decoder must never
have"*. The UID arm copied its shape and not its care. It now refuses with
`plist_int_out_of_range`. Two regression tests, one for the trap and one proving a UID that
does fit still decodes.

Worth noting why the fuzz arm missed it: `PlistOracle` mutates documents **Foundation
wrote**, and Foundation only emits UIDs from `NSKeyedArchiver`, never with the top bit set.
A fuzzer seeded from a well-behaved writer inherits that writer's blind spots.

**The YAML merge key was quadratic — REAL, overstated, fixed.** `mergeInto` linear-scanned
the accumulated pairs for every merged pair. The reader claimed 4.8 s at 80 KB; measured, it
is 62 ms at 107 KB — about 80× off. But at scale the mechanism is exactly as described:

| document | with `<<:` | without |
|---|---|---|
| 219 KB | 0.246 s | 0.002 s |
| 468 KB | 0.872 s | 0.002 s |
| 979 KB | **3.498 s** | 0.005 s |

Time quadruples for every doubling. A 1 MB YAML document with one merge key took three and
a half seconds; the same document with that one line removed took five milliseconds.

Neither guard sees it. The node count stays small, so the alias-expansion budget never
fires, and the nesting is shallow, so `maxDepth` never fires. Both YAML guards watch the
wrong quantity: this bomb is **wide**, not deep or shared.

`YAML.Node` is `Hashable`, so a set gives the identical predicate rather than an
approximation. **3.498 s → 0.015 s**, and linear. Three tests: the timing ceiling, explicit
keys still beating merged ones, and earlier merge sources still beating later ones. The
libyaml differential and the full fuzz suite agree.

**Unbounded XML entity recursion — DID NOT REPRODUCE.** The claim was a stack overflow from
a chain of 2,000 distinct entities. Tested at 500, 2,000, 8,000, 60,000 and 200,000 on the
main thread, and at 8,000 and 20,000 on a 512 KB thread: every one returned a document with
no issues and exit 0. The reported mechanism — budget charged after the recursive call —
is visible in the source, but whatever bounds it in practice, it is not reachable this way.
Recorded as not-a-finding rather than fixed.

**Also claimed and false:** that `CLAUDE.md`'s status table still lists the columnar types as
built. It lists them in a row headed **REMOVED IN FULL**, which is the opposite.

The lesson is the one this repository keeps relearning, now from the other side: an
unverified finding is not a finding. Two of the five claims above would have been wrong to
act on, and one of them was a crash worth dropping everything for.

## 2. The finding under the findings

Three of the eight above are the same shape, and the shape has now appeared five times in
this repository in four days:

> **A check that cannot fail reads exactly like a check that passed.**

The `keypath` gate printed a failure banner and exited 0. The allocation gate's limits were
loose enough that a tripling passed. `RESULTS.md` quoted a number whose source had been
deleted. Earlier in the week: `ValidateBench` divided by a constant it did not measure;
`ColumnDecodable` was recorded "free" from a measurement that could not resolve the
difference.

Every one of them produced *output that looked like verification*. That is the class to
attack, and the attack is cheap: **a gate must be able to fail, and a number must have an
owner in the same run.**

## 3. Verification gaps, in priority order

These are the recommendations, not the fixes. Ordered by what they would have caught.

### 3.1 Nothing compiles the documented examples — **HIGH**

The website compiles every example it shows, and that rule has caught five bugs in two days.
`README.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/EXPERIENCE.md` compile nothing, and that is where the
flagship example rotted. A CI job that extracts ```` ```swift ```` blocks from those three
files, wraps each in a consumer target and builds it would have caught this one on the day
it was introduced. Most blocks are fragments and would need a marker for "this one is
whole" — that is the only real design question.

### 3.2 The three non-JSON parsers never have their error codes asserted — **HIGH**

40 of 120 issue codes are produced by no test: 19 `xml_*`, 8 `yaml_*`, 7 `plist_*`, plus
`not_finite`, `invalid_regex_pattern`, `regex_unavailable`, `pattern_mismatch`,
`date_not_after`. Verified by extracting every `public static let` from
`IssueCode+Names.swift` and grepping the whole test target for each.

`MessageCoverageTests` proves every code *renders* a sentence, which is why this looks
covered and is not — it never produces one. `ParserTests.swift:100-104` is five malformed
XML documents asserted only as `#expect(throws: AssayError.self)`: an XML parser that
collapsed every failure into one code would pass the suite unchanged.

### 3.3 The `@Schema` decode path is never fuzzed — **HIGH**

`DiffFuzz` mutates input into `JSON.Value.decode`, `YAML.decodeAll`, `XML.decode` and
`TOML.decode` — the value models. The only `@Schema` type any oracle reaches is one reading
back Assay's own output. So the window dispatch table, `@Key(path:)` chains, `@Extras`,
`@Inline`, the narrow-integer parsers and the union rewind machinery see no adversarial
bytes. The container-depth leak fixed in `a88e12a` was in exactly that machinery and was
found by reading, not by the fuzzer.

### 3.4 There is no single local command that runs what CI runs — **MEDIUM**

`CONTRIBUTING.md` documents eight, in three sections. The DocC one is in a different section
from the rest, which is precisely the one that was skipped yesterday and went red. A `check`
script that runs build, tests, DocC convert, the allocation gate and the compile-time gate
would make "I ran the checks" mean something.

## 4. Developer experience

Beyond the flagship example, from probes that were compiled and run.

**Malformed input is the one case where the error is worst — CRITICAL.** For a library whose
headline is that it tells you what went wrong, every JSON syntax error is the bare predicate
`is not a well-formed document`, with no subject, never saying what was expected — and
truncated input gets no position at all. `trailingContent` then fires as a redundant second
error at the same column on almost every syntax failure, so the common case reports two
errors for one mistake.

**Forgetting `@Schema` on a nested type lands inside the expansion — CRITICAL.** The
`_assayRequire` assertion fires as designed, but the `_assay` call errors beside it at a line
the user never wrote, naming an underscored internal. Neither message says "add `@Schema` to
`Plain`".

**Missing-capability errors name internal protocols, never the fix — MAJOR.**
`parse(yaml:)` without `formats:` says `requires that 'A7' conform to 'RawDecodable'`. Same
for `parse(body:)`, `jsonSchema(for:)` and `.encoded(as:)`. Compare `@XML(root:)`, whose
refusal says "Add `formats: .xml` (or `.all`)" — that is the standard the others should meet.
A related surprise found while probing: a default JSON-only `@Schema` **cannot** use
`parse(body:contentType:accepting: [.json])` at all, because that door requires `formats:` to
name a non-JSON format. Undocumented.

**Four attribute combinations are still silently accepted — MAJOR.** Verified by running
them: `@Key(path:)` + `@Inline` (the path is dropped), `@Coerce` on a nested `@Schema`,
`@XML(.attribute)` on an array (decodes `"a b"` to one unsplit element), `@Key` + `@Extras`.
The first is the same bug `SchemaRefusals` already refuses for `@Key("x")` + `@Key(path:)`.

**`@Check` and `@AsyncCheck` have different arities — MAJOR.** `@Check(\Type.field)` takes a
key path; `@AsyncCheck` takes none. Writing the sibling by analogy gives four errors and a
warning, two of them inside the expansion.

**`@Validate` type refusals point at the `@Schema` line, not the field — MAJOR.** Every other
refusal — `@Preprocess`, `@OneOrMany`, `Set`, `Data`, `URL`, `Decimal`, `var x = 3` — points
at the property.

**An invalid string escape still produces the doubled message the 2026-09-10 fix claimed to
remove.** That fix set `escapeErrorAt` only in the `\uXXXX` arm; the `default:` arm still
returns `nil` with the cursor mid-string.

**What is genuinely excellent, and should not be touched.** `Set`/`Data`/`URL`/`Decimal`/
tuple/`Any`/IUO/`[T?]` refusals each name the replacement spelling. `var x = 3`, empty
`@Schema`, generic struct, `@Ignore`+`@Validate`, `@Extras`+`.reject`, `@Inline` on a
non-nested type, `@Check` in an extension, `@Schema` on a class — all refused at the right
line with the fix named. Errors print their caret render from `print`, interpolation,
`localizedDescription` and even a `try!` trap. All four prior-audit findings are fixed.

## 5. Organisation

No file is wrong, but three are doing more than one job, and the seams are already marked.

- `AssayMacros/CodeGen.swift` (894) is three things its own MARKs name — body assembly,
  dispatch emission, per-field emission — plus two general-purpose `String` helpers at the
  bottom used by three other files, where nobody would look for them.
- `AssayCore/DateParser.swift` (734): RFC 9110 (220 lines) and the pattern interpreter (175)
  are independent format families.
- `AssayXML/XMLParser.swift` (865) is the only parser not following its own module's
  `+Section` convention; entities and namespaces extract along existing boundaries.
- `ValidateGen.swift` (116) and `ValidationGen.swift` (321) are confusably named for
  unrelated jobs.
- `Tests/ParserTests.swift` (546) is two parsers under an inaccurate name;
  `DecodeTests.swift` (458) has five bolted-on suites and 50 lines of duplication justified
  by a premise `KeyStyleTests.swift:15` disproves.
- `JSON.Value` and `RawValue` carry ~120 lines of the same hand-copied accessor surface with
  nothing keeping them in step.

Checked and **correctly factored**, so they should be left alone: `AssayReader.swift`,
`Messages.swift`, `UnionGen.swift`, `Decode.swift`, `Assay/Macros.swift`, the four
Node→`RawValue` projections, both writer families, `AssayFoundation`.

Cheapest structural win: `Sources/Assay/Assay.swift` carries a file-map for its 13 files;
`AssayCore` has 33 and none. Add the map before adding directories.
